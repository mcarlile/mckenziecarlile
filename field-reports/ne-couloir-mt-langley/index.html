N.E. Couloir of Mt. Langley

Sierra Nevada · Ski Descent · Spring 2024

Mt. Langley sits at the southern end of the Sierra Nevada crest, just outside the main Sequoia National Park boundary. At 14,032 feet it's the southernmost fourteener in the range, and the northeast couloir — a narrow, sustained chute dropping nearly 2,000 vertical feet — is one of the finest ski descents in California.

We skinned in from Horseshoe Meadow in the dark, headlamps cutting small cones through the pre-dawn cold. The approach is long but straightforward: a trail through lodgepole pines, then open slopes leading toward the couloir entrance at roughly 12,500 feet. By sunrise we were booting up the lower apron, crampons biting into firm spring névé.

The Couloir

The northeast aspect keeps the snow in excellent condition well into May. On the day we climbed it, the upper two-thirds were a consistent 40–45 degrees — steep enough to demand attention, mellow enough to ski with confidence in good conditions. The crux is a short section near the top where the couloir narrows and the consequence of a fall increases. We roped up here and moved one at a time.

The ski down was everything we'd hoped for: smooth, sun-softened corn from the top, transitioning to slightly heavier snow lower. We made wide, arcing turns and let out the kind of whoops that echo off granite faces. Back at the cars by early afternoon, sun-beaten and deeply satisfied.

Conditions change fast in the Sierra — a warm week can turn excellent corn to garbage slush overnight. Check avalanche forecasts, get an early start, and be prepared to turn around. The mountain will be there next year.

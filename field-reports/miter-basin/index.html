Miter Basin

Sierra Nevada · Backpacking · September 2023

Miter Basin sits at the end of a long walk in the southern Sierra — six miles and 3,000 feet of gain from the Crabtree Meadows trailhead. The basin itself is a wide granite bowl, studded with tarns, ringed by peaks above 13,000 feet, and almost entirely above tree line. It's the kind of place that makes you wonder how it isn't more famous, until you remember how far you had to walk to get there.

We came in September, past the crowds of summer, chasing the last warm nights of the year before the high country closed under snow. The light in September is softer and more golden than summer, and the basin photographed beautifully in the hour after sunrise.

The Basin

From a base camp at the lower tarn, we spent three days exploring: a scramble to the summit of Mt. McAdie, a long wander around the upper lakes, afternoons reading in the sun on warm granite slabs. The fishing in the tarns was poor — these high lakes don't support much — but that was fine. The point was to be there.

On the last morning, the temperature dropped overnight and we woke to a thin glaze of ice on the tent and a dusting of snow on the surrounding peaks. Autumn in the Sierra happens fast. We packed up and walked out under a sky that looked like it had been scoured clean.

Miter Basin sits within the John Muir Wilderness. Permits are required for overnight stays; quota fills quickly for summer weekends but September is usually open. Hang your food — this is bear country.

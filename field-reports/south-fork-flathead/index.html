South Fork of the Flathead

Montana · Packrafting · Summer 2023

The South Fork of the Flathead drains the Bob Marshall Wilderness — one of the largest roadless areas in the contiguous United States. The river runs clear and cold through a valley that feels genuinely remote: grizzly bears, mountain goats, and a silence that takes days to fully settle into.

We flew into a private airstrip at the headwaters and spent nine days floating out, fishing every morning and making camp on gravel bars each evening. Packrafts kept the raft weight to a minimum, which meant we could carry enough food and camera gear without suffering under punishing loads.

The Float

The upper river is technical — several Class III rapids require scouting and a few short portages in lower water. By mid-river the gradient eases and the valley opens into broad meadows grazed by elk. We saw more wildlife in nine days than in any previous trip: three grizzlies, two black bears, dozens of whitetails and elk, a wolverine crossing a snowfield above camp on day four.

The fishing was exceptional. Westslope cutthroat on dry flies, almost every evening, in water so clear you could count the fish holding in a pool from the bank. We kept nothing — the South Fork has been protected from stocking, and those fish deserve to stay wild.

Permits are required for the Bob Marshall Wilderness during peak season. The logistics are involved — charter flights, shuttle coordination — but the payoff is a river that feels untouched in a way that's increasingly rare.

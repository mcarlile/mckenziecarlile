Product Design

IBM Watson Data Platform

Designing governance tools that help enterprise teams understand, trust, and protect the data powering their AI and analytics models.

Role UX Designer, Governance Squad
Timeline Early 2017 – Dec 2017
Team 60 designers, researchers & engineers

Watson Data Platform is IBM's cloud-native environment for industrial-scale data science — a unified product combining metadata cataloging, collaborative projects, data refinery, pipeline development, notebook-based analysis, and machine learning model training. I joined the governance squad in early 2017, focused on the tools that help enterprise teams manage the quality, compliance, and trustworthiness of their data assets.

The problem

Data science models are only as good as the data they consume. Poor quality data, incomplete metadata, and compliance gaps create real risk — in regulated industries, the consequences can be severe. Our squad asked: How might we build governance tools that enable teams to understand, trust, and protect the data their analytic assets ingest?

Through research at IBM's Chief Data Officer Summit and interviews with customers and internal teams, we identified four compounding pain points.

Data discovery

Data consumers spend disproportionate time searching for data rather than analyzing it. Assets scattered across company warehouses make finding relevant data — and understanding ownership and access requirements — slow and opaque.

Data understanding

Understanding dataset context requires "tribal knowledge." Technical metadata lacks business clarity, forcing data stewards to manually profile data column-by-column to bridge the gap between technical systems and business meaning.

Policy enforcement

Governance teams can document policies but struggle to enforce them. Most rely on expensive manual auditing. Real-time compliance monitoring simply doesn't exist in accessible form. The gap between documentation and enforcement is wide.

Accessibility

Legacy governance tools were built for governance teams, not data scientists. The interfaces are IT-focused and overwhelming — showing so much information that they become unhelpful to the people who need them most.

Process

Research & synthesis

Our research spanned generative interviews, observations at IBM's Chief Data Officer Summit, Forrester and Gartner analyst reports, and competitive documentation review. A key finding was the emergence of the Chief Data Officer as a critical persona — an executive bridging data infrastructure and business units, managing compliance while unlocking data's value.

Cross-squad, six design researchers participated in a week-long synthesis workshop to cluster and organize data points across the platform's six capability areas.

Information architecture & sketching

I created user flow diagrams mapping navigation across three core governance tools and developed sitemaps showing hierarchy and platform connections. Remote card-sorting activities with sponsor users helped prioritize which business term attributes mattered most — reducing 50+ legacy attributes to an essential manageable set.

Mid-fidelity wireframes

Three governance tools

Policy manager

The policy manager lets CDO office staff build complex governance rules without writing code, powered by IBM's policy enforcement engine. I designed around the full lifecycle of a policy — draft, active, archive — and explored how rules nest within policies and categories, giving governance teams a path from static documentation to active enforcement.

Business glossary

The business glossary connects business terminology to the technical metadata in dataset columns. By surfacing business context upfront, data scientists no longer need to decipher cryptic database names or rely on colleagues for institutional knowledge.

Governance dashboard

The dashboard gives Chief Data Officers a single view of governance program health — policy enforcement rates, violations, most-used business terms, and overall coverage. The key design question: what does a CDO need to see to feel confident their catalog is compliant?

"The more I work with data and metadata, the more I see the whole world as a graph. I want a single pane of glass."

Outcome

The public open beta launched in December 2017, delivering all three governance experiences. Post-launch research surfaced several clear next steps: users consistently requested approval and review workflows, organizations needed graceful paths to migrate from legacy on-premises governance systems, and testing confirmed that the information-dense tabular interface served governance teams well but alienated data consumers — pointing toward role-differentiated experiences in the next design cycle.

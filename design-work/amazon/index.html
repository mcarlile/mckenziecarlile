Product Design

Amazon

A browser extension concept helping Prime members discover the full value of their subscription — surfacing relevant music, video, and book content exactly when they're searching for it.

Role UX Design Intern
Timeline Summer 2014
Team Browser Integration Technology

Amazon Prime offered a growing suite of digital benefits — Prime Music, Kindle Lending Library, Amazon Instant Video — but many subscribers didn't know what they had access to, let alone how to use it. The existing 1Button browser extension handled price comparisons for physical goods, but left the entire media side of Prime untouched. My internship project explored how to change that.

Image — Extension concept overview

The problem

Prime is a compelling subscription on paper, but its value is easy to underestimate when benefits are scattered across disconnected surfaces. A member searching for a book, movie, or album elsewhere on the web has no signal that they might already have access through Prime. The opportunity was clear: meet members where they already are, at the moment of intent, and show them what's already theirs.

"Leverage Amazon's digital media services to increase the value of a Prime subscription — and surface surprisingly relevant, accurate contextual suggestions."

Process

Mapping the use cases

I started by mapping user scenarios across the three primary content types — books, video, and music — and across different web contexts where searches happen: general web searches, entertainment news articles, review sites, and retailer pages. Each context warranted different extension behavior and different content prioritization.

A critical complexity was account state. The extension needed to respond differently based on Prime membership status, purchase history, and content availability. A member who already owns a title should see something different than one who hasn't. I defined conditional logic for the major account states before moving into flows and wireframes.

Image — User scenario mapping

Flows & wireframes

With use cases defined, I worked through user flows covering the range of trigger contexts and content types, then translated these into wireframes. The design challenge was keeping the extension lightweight and non-intrusive — helpful when relevant, invisible when not. I applied Amazon's established visual design language to bring the wireframes to a prototype-ready state.

Image — Wireframes and user flows

Solution

The result was a contextual browser extension that detects when a Prime member is viewing or searching for media content, then surfaces relevant suggestions from their subscription. Responses are tailored to individual membership status and content eligibility — a member without Prime sees a conversion prompt, while an active subscriber sees what they can access right now.

Image — Final prototype

Outcome

The project aimed to boost Prime awareness, increase conversion, and drive engagement with underutilized digital benefits. The prototype was approved for public sharing by my internship managers. Specific shipment details are covered by NDA.
